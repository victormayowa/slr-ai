# Certainty of evidence and interpretation

## GRADE

Each outcome with final results gets a GRADE assessment. Suggested ratings come from the analysis and risk of bias:
the share of weight from studies at high risk, heterogeneity and prediction intervals, confidence intervals against no
effect and the optimal information size, and small-study effects. A methodologist rates every domain (risk of bias,
inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision, publication bias, and for observational evidence the upgrading domains),
justifies any rating up or down, and signs the assessment off.

## Summary of findings

The summary of findings table shows relative effects, absolute effects per 1000 for the baseline risks you enter,
numbers needed to treat (with Altman's intervals), certainty, and GRADE informative statements. Export it as CSV or
Word, or in a GRADEpro-style evidence profile layout.

## Evidence to Decision

Evidence to Decision frameworks record judgments on the GRADE criteria (problem, desirable and undesirable effects,
certainty, values, balance, resources, cost-effectiveness, equity, acceptability, feasibility) and the recommendation.

## Prior reviews

Add earlier reviews by DOI to compare study overlap (the corrected covered area) and see where conclusions changed.

## Interpretation

Informative statements and limitations are drafted from the GRADE ratings. The AI can draft a plain-language summary;
any number in it that isn't in the summary of findings blocks approval. A clinical expert approves interpretive text.
